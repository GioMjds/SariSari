# Channel: LinkedIn

## Role in Mix

Secondary. LinkedIn is where the founder / builder / OFW audience reads
longer-form writing. We do not push product here as often as we push
thinking, decisions, and stories. Post less, post longer.

## Audience on This Channel

- Primary: Filipino indie builders, dev community, agency folks
- Secondary: OFW professionals (HK, Singapore, Riyadh, etc.) funding a
  store back home
- Tertiary: investors and curious lurkers who may one day write about us
  or partner up

## Posting Window

Best times:

- 8:00 - 10:00 AM, Tuesday through Thursday
- Avoid Mondays and weekends for founder-led content
- One post per week is plenty. Two at the absolute max during launches.

## Caption Format

- First line is the hook. It has to make sense to someone who is not in
  our DMs. No "Guys" / "So I" / "Excited to share." Just the sentence.
- 800-1200 characters for founder-led posts. LinkedIn truncates at ~210
  on mobile, so the first three lines matter most.
- Single-line breaks between paragraphs. Walls of text die on LinkedIn.
- A personal stake beats a product update every time.

## Hashtag Set

3-5 only. Put them at the bottom, not inline.

```text
#IndieHackers #BuildInPublic #FilipinoTech #ReactNative
#SmallBusiness #FilipinoEntrepreneur
```

Skip generic tags (`#Startup`, `#Tech`) - they drown us in noise.

## Visual Specs

- 1200x627 (landscape link preview) for any post with a link
- 1080x1080 (square) or 1080x1350 (portrait) for image-only posts
- No carousels here - LinkedIn's carousel format is clunky; one strong
  image beats five slides for our audience.

## What Works Here

- Decision posts ("Why we chose SQLite over Supabase")
- Numbers posts with one clear takeaway ("How 14 beta stores changed
  the POS in two months")
- Process posts ("How we run stocktake in 30 minutes instead of a day")
- Story posts that center the user, not the founder
- Replies that add value to other founders' posts - this is half the
  strategy

## What Does Not Work Here

- Fake humble brags ("I almost failed but here is what I learned")
- Engagement bait ("Comment 'YES' if you agree")
- Tagging 30 people
- Reposting Facebook content verbatim

## Sample Post

> Most sari-sari stores do not have a tablet.
>
> They do not have a barcode scanner, a credit-card reader, or a backup
> internet connection. They have a mid-range Android phone that drops
> signal twice a day, a notebook that has seen better days, and a
> memory for who owes what that is genuinely impressive.
>
> We are building SariSari for that phone. Offline-first is not a
> feature on the marketing page - it is the architecture. The whole app
> is one SQLite database on the device. There is no "sync". There is no
> "cloud backup". There is a database, and the owner of the store can
> trust what it says.
>
> Sometimes the constraint you design around is the one your user is
> already living inside.
>
> #BuildInPublic #IndieHackers #FilipinoTech

## Response Patterns

- Comments asking for technical depth: write a paragraph reply. It is
  your stage. Other founders will read it.
- Comments pushing back / disagreeing: thank, disagree with substance,
  never get defensive.
- Connection requests from strangers (especially OFWs): accept and
  follow up with a real message. This channel is gold for that audience.
